How North Charleston Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep vegetation trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and have a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

How old is your system? That number tells you a lot. Most well-maintained air conditioners make it to 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is beyond that range and also shows rising bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or repeated repairs, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement in peak season.

Minor issues don't stay minor when they're ignored. Consider a weak capacitor: swapping it out is a quick, affordable repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a few miserable nights.
